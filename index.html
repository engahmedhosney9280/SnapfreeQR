How QR codes work

A QR code is a small grid of black and white squares that stores information in a pattern a camera can read — much more of it than a barcode, and from any angle.

01

Encode

Your text or link is converted into a sequence of bits, then arranged into the grid pattern, with extra data added so the code can still be read even if part of it is damaged or covered.

02

Share

Print the code, add it to a poster, or drop it into a document or email. Since the code just encodes text, it works anywhere an image can go.

03

Scan

A phone camera or scanner app locates the three corner squares to orient the grid, reads the pattern, and reconstructs your original link or text instantly.

Frequently asked questions

Is this actually free to use?

Yes. There's no sign-up, no watermark, and no limit on how many codes you generate.

Do QR codes expire?

No. A QR code made here simply stores your text or link directly in the pattern, so it works for as long as the link itself is valid — there's no third-party service that can shut it off.

Do you store what I type in?

No. The code is generated entirely in your browser, so your text never gets sent to a server or saved anywhere.

Will a colored code still scan properly?

Most colors scan fine as long as there's strong contrast against the white background. Very light or pastel colors can be harder for some cameras to pick up, so darker tones are the safer choice.

Can I use these codes commercially?

Yes, feel free to use your downloaded codes for personal or business purposes, including on products and printed materials.

What size should I download for printing?

For anything printed and scanned from a short distance, the large option gives you enough resolution to stay crisp; for a business card or small label, medium is usually plenty.
